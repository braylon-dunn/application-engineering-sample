# Customer-Facing Technical Walkthrough — Script + Slide Outline

**Type:** Newly authored fictional presentation / narrated video script (approximately 3 minutes).

## Slide 1 — What are we sizing?
**On screen:** Fictional factory single-line overview: utility transformer → main breaker → 480 V bus → three feeders.
**Narration:** “Today we're reviewing a preliminary low-voltage switchgear concept for a manufacturing facility. The goal is to agree on service conditions and load requirements before selecting a final OEM configuration.”

## Slide 2 — Translating requirements into a lineup
**On screen:** 2,000 A main bus; 800 A, 600 A and 400 A feeder placeholders; space for expansion.
**Narration:** “The initial scope calls for a 2,000-amp bus with three feeder positions. These numbers are placeholders, not final selections. We'll reconcile them against the load schedule, the latest one-line and the equipment room layout.”

## Slide 3 — The questions that change the quote
**On screen:** Fault current, breaker interrupting rating, working clearance, cable entry, communications, lead time.
**Narration:** “A quote can look complete and still miss the most important requirements. Before we release pricing, we need the utility fault-current information, protective coordination expectations, installation constraints and metering requirements.”

## Slide 4 — Risk and safety checkpoints
**On screen:** OEM ratings → coordination study → arc-flash assessment → approved submittals.
**Narration:** “We cannot confirm the lineup's short-circuit suitability from bus ampacity alone. Qualified engineering review and the manufacturer's verified ratings are essential. We also need to understand the coordination and arc-flash study scope.”

## Slide 5 — Clear next steps
**On screen:** RFI log, assumptions list, OEM quote, revised proposal, customer approval.
**Narration:** “The next step is to close the open questions, validate a buildable configuration with the OEM, and provide a transparent commercial proposal that clearly separates included equipment from options and exclusions.”

**Production notes:** Use a simple animated one-line graphic, highlighted callouts for each RFI, and a final checklist. No customer logos or proprietary diagrams are used.
