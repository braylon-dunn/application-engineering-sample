# Sample: Low-Voltage Switchgear Technical Proposal

**Status:** Illustrative demonstration only — fictional customer and requirements.

## 1. Project brief
A fictional manufacturing site requests one indoor 480Y/277 V, 3-phase, 4-wire, 60 Hz low-voltage distribution lineup serving a 2,000 A service with a main breaker and three feeder breakers. The facility requires future expansion capacity and a utility outage coordination review.

## 2. Preliminary scope matrix
| Requirement | Preliminary response | Validation |
|---|---|---|
| Service | 480Y/277 V, 3-phase, 4-wire | Confirm source and grounding |
| Main bus | 2,000 A | Verify load study and OEM thermal ratings |
| Main breaker | 2,000 A drawout, electronic trip | Confirm interrupting rating and protection settings |
| Feeders | 800 A, 600 A, 400 A | Verify cable and load schedules |
| Short-circuit rating | TBD | Utility fault-current letter and study required |
| Enclosure | Indoor, manufacturer-standard | Verify space, ingress and seismic requirements |
| Metering | Digital main metering allowance | Confirm utility and communications protocol |

## 3. Commercial quotation structure
Line items: main section, feeder sections, bus and enclosure options, metering, factory testing, documentation, freight, commissioning support (optional), and recommended spares. Prices and delivery dates intentionally omitted pending OEM quotations and approved scope.

## 4. Clarifications / RFIs
1. Provide available utility fault current at the service point and transformer impedance.
2. Confirm selective coordination requirements and whether an arc-flash study is available.
3. Provide the latest single-line diagram, equipment room layout, working clearances and cable entry requirements.
4. Confirm applicable listing, seismic, accessibility, metering and communications requirements.
5. Clarify generator/ATS interface, if any, and future expansion space.

## 5. Assumptions and exclusions
This sample assumes an indoor installation and no paralleling of sources. Excludes field installation, cables, utility approval, protective coordination study, arc-flash study, permit drawings, and final engineering signoff unless explicitly contracted.

## 6. Review gates
Customer approves scope → OEM confirms product configuration and ratings → engineering checks studies and interfaces → revised commercial proposal → submittal approval → release to manufacturing.

**Safety note:** Ratings and breaker selections above are only placeholders for demonstrating proposal workflow, not recommended selections for an actual installation.
